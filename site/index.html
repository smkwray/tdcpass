Treasury Contribution to Deposits

How TDC arithmetic changes across flow and deposit perimeters.

TDC Pass compares a quarterly Treasury Deposit Component proxy with named deposit outcomes, then shows how the comparison changes with measurement, perimeter, clock, and counterpart series. It is an arithmetic and boundary comparator, not a separate estimator or causal evidence chain.

Research scope

What TDC Pass measures.

Source-flow input

The quarterly TDC proxy enters as an upstream measured object.

The comparator takes the published quarterly proxy and its sensitivity variants as inputs. It does not create a second estimate or elevate one measurement variant through the comparison itself.

Deposit-outcome boundary

Each comparison names the deposit claim being measured.

The covered domestic nonbank deposit-claim perimeter is distinct from the settlement-system complement. Loan, mortgage, and broader holder-asset series are sensitivity comparisons, not interchangeable definitions of the matched outcome.

Read the two objects separately. The TDC proxy names the source-flow perimeter; the deposit series names the outcome perimeter. Their arithmetic difference is useful for comparison and diagnostics, but it is not an independently estimated deposit-creation channel.

TDC measurement ladder

The source-flow definition materially changes the comparison.

The corrected sensitivity ladder shows how interest, fiscal, and depository-perimeter choices change the upstream TDC input. It therefore measures definition sensitivity rather than adjudicating a preferred estimator.

Why the headline remains visible. The headline and corrected Tier 2 and Tier 3 rows answer different measurement questions. Displaying them together makes the sensitivity transparent without treating arithmetic agreement as validation.
What the sign difference means. The headline and corrected rows can imply different signs. If the corrections better match the intended source-flow definition, the corrected rows show how much of the headline comparison depends on measurement scope. They do not, by themselves, determine which counterpart absorbed the difference.

Deposit-outcome boundary

Loan-component comparisons are sensitivities, not a second evidence chain.

The displayed loan-core coefficients are negative at h0, h4, and h8, while the broader loan-plus-corporate bridge is positive at h0. If these series track stable parts of the covered deposit perimeter, the pattern is consistent with different portfolio components moving in opposite directions. It does not identify a complete deposit-flow decomposition.

Boundary checks and comparison tiers

Some measured series sit outside the covered deposit-claim perimeter.

Different claim boundaries

Treasury and rest-of-world deposit series

These series are observed and economically relevant, but they do not directly measure the covered domestic nonbank deposit claim.

They remain useful boundary diagnostics. If a future design supplies the same clock and claim incidence, their relationship to the covered outcome can be tested directly.

Also excluded

Trade, QE, offshore routing

  • Exports and imports are economically relevant but are not same-scope deposit-settlement measures.
  • Fed MBS and QE transactions require seller-incidence information before they can be mapped to the covered claim.
  • Offshore, correspondent, and foreign-bank series provide external counterpart context without assigning a source or destination of funds.

Comparison hierarchy

How the rows are used

  • Matched arithmetic: a named source-flow proxy against a named deposit outcome
  • Outcome sensitivities: loan components and the broader loan-plus-corporate bridge
  • Boundary diagnostics: broader private, Treasury, rest-of-world, and settlement-system complement series

Time variation

The h0 comparison varies less than the later-horizon path.

The h0 total-deposit coefficient is more stable across the displayed periods than the medium-horizon coefficients. The COVID and post-COVID window has the largest h0 response. If the measurement relationship is comparable across periods, that difference is consistent with state-dependent absorption; the period split does not identify the state mechanism.

Counterpart-series context

Conditional patterns suggest where to look next.

These coefficients compare the TDC proxy with lending, external, funding, and counterpart-asset series. Foreign nontransaction movements are most visible at h0, while lending movements become more pronounced at later horizons. If the mappings are stable, that sequence is consistent with an early external adjustment followed by slower portfolio movement; it does not assign transaction-level incidence.

Reproducibility and interpretation

The comparison is reproducible and its scope is explicit.

The imported shock and displayed conditional coefficients pass the reported quality checks. The arithmetic, sensitivity, and counterpart patterns can therefore be reproduced as shown. Causal incidence, portfolio mechanism, and stock-response interpretations require designs outside this comparator.
